# Underwriting Workflow

PRC-002 sets the 8 stages Underwriting follows from file assignment to decision. A file ends in one of four outcomes: approve, approve with conditions, decline or refer to exception. Each stage below names who acts, what is checked and which policy applies.

## Stage 1: File assignment

A complete file released by Mortgage Operations (see PRC-001) is assigned to an Underwriter by the Underwriting team lead, who considers the Underwriter's current workload and the loan amount. The team lead assigns a file with a loan amount above 750,000 to a Senior Underwriter, because that is above the Underwriter authority in PRC-004. The assigned Underwriter becomes the owner of the file and is named in the loan system. The owner records the date of assignment and checks that the file has a loan number in the format DMC-YYYY-NNNNN. An Underwriter must not accept a file in which they have a personal interest, and must tell the team lead so that the file can be reassigned. A file that is reassigned keeps its loan number, and the new owner reviews the notes of the earlier owner before continuing.

## Stage 2: Completeness check

The Underwriter checks that every document listed on the application is in the file, is legible and is complete, with no page missing. This check repeats the check by Mortgage Operations, because Underwriting is responsible for the credit decision. If a document is missing or unclear, the Underwriter lists the item and sends the request to the broker or the branch through the loan system. The file waits in a pending-documents status until the items arrive. The Underwriter may not decide a file on a document that is missing, may not accept a verbal statement in place of a document, and may not rely on a document that looks altered. If a document looks altered or does not agree with the other documents in the file, the Underwriter must stop the file, raise a fraud flag and refer it as set out in POL-010, because a fraud flag can never be excepted.

## Stage 3: Income verification

The Underwriter verifies the income of each borrower using the documents required by POL-002 for salaried, variable and rental income, and POL-008 for self-employed income. For a salaried borrower, the Underwriter checks the pay stub and an employment letter dated within 30 days of the decision. For variable income the Underwriter calculates the 2-year average. For rental income the Underwriter counts 50% of gross rents. The Underwriter records each document, the amount counted and the method in the file. Income that cannot be verified is not counted, and the Underwriter tells the broker or branch which document is still needed. If the borrower cannot supply a required income document, the only route is an income-document exception under POL-010, which is decided by the role named in PRC-005.

## Stage 4: Credit and debt review

The Underwriter reviews the credit bureau report for each borrower and lists every debt that must be counted in the TDS ratio under POL-003. The Underwriter compares the debts on the application with the debts on the credit bureau report and asks the borrower to explain any difference. A debt that appears on the report but not on the application must be counted unless proof of payment is in the file. The Underwriter notes late payments, collections and other items that are relevant to the risk, and records the explanation of the borrower. These items may be used later as compensating factors or as reasons to decline, as described in POL-010. The Underwriter may not ignore a debt because the borrower says it will be paid off, unless the payment is a condition of the approval and the lawyer's instructions show it.

## Stage 5: Property and appraisal review

The Underwriter checks that the property meets POL-006: an eligible type, within Canada, with one to four units, and occupancy that fits the product. Mortgage Operations orders the full appraisal from an approved appraiser, and the Underwriter reads it in full. The Underwriter confirms that the property description in the appraisal agrees with the purchase agreement and the application. The value used for loan-to-value is the lower of the purchase price and the appraised value. If the appraisal is lower than the purchase price, the Underwriter tells the broker or branch that the borrower must reduce the loan or increase the down payment from accepted sources under POL-005. A property that does not meet POL-006 is declined, because property eligibility cannot be excepted.

## Stage 6: Policy tests on ratios, LTV and down payment

The Underwriter calculates the qualifying rate under POL-004, then the GDS and TDS ratios under POL-003, the loan-to-value ratio under POL-007 and the minimum down payment under POL-005. The ratios must be at or below 35% for GDS and 42% for TDS. The loan-to-value must be at or below the conventional limit of 80% or be covered by mortgage default insurance, and it must also be within any lower product limit. The maximum amortization is 25 years for an insured mortgage and 30 years for an uninsured mortgage. The Underwriter records every result and the figures used. A file that passes every test goes to the authority check. A file that fails a test that can be excepted goes to the authority check with the failed test noted. A file that fails a test that cannot be excepted is declined.

## Stage 7: Authority check

The Underwriter checks that the loan amount is within the authority of the role that will approve the file. An Underwriter may approve a loan amount up to 750,000, a Senior Underwriter up to 1,500,000, and the Credit Committee any amount above that (see PRC-004). If the amount is above the Underwriter's authority, the file is passed to a Senior Underwriter or to the Credit Committee for approval. If a policy test has failed and the failure can be excepted, the Underwriter prepares an EXC-100 under PRC-005, with the loan number, the exception type, the requested value, the justification and the compensating factors. The Underwriter recommends but does not approve any exception. The file stays in a pending-approval status until a human with the right authority approves the exception. Nobody may approve an exception verbally.

## Stage 8: Decision and outcome

The role with authority records the decision in the loan system, and the outcome must be one of four. Approve: the file meets every policy and the loan is approved as requested. Approve with conditions: the file is approved subject to conditions that must be met before closing (see PRC-003). Decline: the file does not meet policy and cannot be excepted, or the exception was refused, and the decision is recorded with the reason. Refer to exception: a test that can be excepted has failed, and the file is sent to the approver named in PRC-005 under form EXC-100. Underwriting tells the broker or branch of the outcome in plain language under PRC-009, and cites the policy that applies to a decline. Mortgage Operations then prepares the file for closing under PRC-006.
