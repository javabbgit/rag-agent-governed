# Rate Holds and Rate Locks

RATE-003 sets the rate hold periods: 120 days for a purchase and 60 days for a refinance. An extension needs an exception under POL-010 plus the extension fee. A rate hold is not an approval.

## Rate hold period for a purchase

A rate hold on a purchase lasts 120 days. A rate hold locks the contract rate of the product the borrower asked for, as shown on the rate sheet in force on the day the hold begins (see RATE-001). A rate hold on a purchase begins on the date Mortgage Operations acknowledges the complete application under PRC-001, and it ends 120 days later. If the purchase closes within the 120 days, the borrower gets the held rate. If it does not close within the 120 days, the hold ends. Without a rate hold, the rate is the rate on the rate sheet in force on the funding date. The qualifying rate of a purchase with a rate hold is calculated from the held rate (see POL-004). A rate hold applies to one product, and a borrower who changes product loses the hold for the new product.

## Rate hold period for a refinance

A rate hold on a refinance lasts 60 days. A refinance is a new mortgage that replaces an existing mortgage, or that advances new money on a property the borrower already owns. A rate hold locks the contract rate of the product the borrower asked for, as shown on the rate sheet in force on the day the hold begins (see RATE-001). The hold begins on the date Mortgage Operations acknowledges the complete application under PRC-001, and it ends 60 days later. If the refinance closes within the 60 days, the borrower gets the held rate. If it does not, the hold ends. Without a rate hold, the rate is the rate on the rate sheet in force on the funding date. A borrower who needs more time than 60 days must ask for an extension under the rules below.

## What a rate hold locks

A rate hold locks the contract rate and nothing else. It does not lock the amount of the loan, the loan-to-value, the qualifying result, the fees or the approval. For a variable-rate product such as VR5-C or HEL-1, a rate hold locks the spread to DMC Prime and the starting rate for the hold period only, and the rate then changes with DMC Prime as the product document describes. A rate hold does not lock the rate of a different product. If the borrower changes the loan amount, the property or the product during the hold, Mortgage Operations tells the borrower whether the hold still applies. Staff must describe a rate hold only as a lock on the rate for the period, and must not describe it as a guarantee of anything else.

## A rate hold is not an approval

A rate hold is not an approval of the mortgage. A borrower may have a rate hold on a file that is later declined, and the hold does not oblige Demo Mortgage Co. to lend. The file is still assessed under every credit policy, and Underwriting decides it under PRC-002. Staff must say this in any message that mentions a rate hold (see PRC-009). A borrower who closes within the hold period gets the held rate only if the mortgage is approved and every condition is met. A rate hold that ends before the borrower has met the conditions does not become an approval and does not entitle the borrower to the rate on the rate sheet in force when it began.

## Extending a rate hold

A rate hold may be extended only through an exception under POL-010. The Underwriter raises an EXC-100 with the loan number, the exception type, the requested new end date, the justification and the compensating factors (see PRC-005), and the exception is decided by the role named in POL-010. An approved extension also needs the rate hold extension fee of $150 in RATE-002, unless the Credit Committee waives it. The extension applies only to the loan and the dates named on the EXC-100. If the exception is not approved, the hold ends on its original date. A delay in closing does not extend a rate hold, and a rate hold is not extended by silence or by a verbal agreement. Nobody may promise a borrower that an extension will be granted.
