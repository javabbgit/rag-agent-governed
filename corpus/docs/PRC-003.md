# Conditions and Document Checklist

PRC-003 lists the standard conditions Underwriting attaches to an approval, the document checklist for each file type, the rule that conditions are due 10 business days before closing, and the role that signs off each condition type.

## Standard conditions

Underwriting attaches conditions to an approval when a fact must still be confirmed before the loan is funded. The standard conditions are: a satisfactory full appraisal of the property (see POL-006); proof that the down payment comes from accepted sources and, for a gift, the signed gift letter (see POL-005); an employment letter dated within 30 days where income depends on it (see POL-002); confirmation from the mortgage default insurer where the loan needs insurance (see POL-007); proof of home insurance on the property; payout statements and discharge of any debt or mortgage that must be paid from the proceeds; and the lawyer's confirmation of clear title. Underwriting may add a condition that is specific to the file, but each condition must be written in plain words, must name the document needed and must name the person who has to supply it. A condition may not be vague or open-ended.

## Conditions due 10 business days before closing

Every condition must be met and the supporting document must be in the loan system at least 10 business days before the closing date. The Underwriter states the due date on the approval, calculated from the closing date in the purchase agreement. Mortgage Operations tracks the due date and sends a reminder to the broker or branch. The due date applies to the document being received, not to the borrower promising to provide it. If a document arrives after the due date, the file owner may still accept it but must tell Mortgage Operations at once, because the closing may have to be moved. Conditions that depend on events after the due date, such as the lawyer's final report on title, are listed separately as closing conditions and are checked under PRC-006. Staff may not tell a borrower that a late condition will not affect the closing.

## Who signs off each condition type

Each condition is signed off by one role, recorded in the loan system with the date. The table sets out the role for each condition type.

| Condition type | Signed off by |
| --- | --- |
| Appraisal | Underwriter |
| Down payment source and gift letter | Underwriter |
| Income documents and employment letter | Underwriter |
| Mortgage default insurance confirmation | Underwriter |
| Home insurance proof | Mortgage Operations |
| Payout statements and discharges | Mortgage Operations |
| Title confirmation from the lawyer | Mortgage Operations |
| Conditions set by an exception approval | The role that approved the exception (see PRC-005) |

A person may not sign off a condition they supplied themselves. A Senior Underwriter signs off in place of the Underwriter on any file the Senior Underwriter approved.

## Document checklist by file type

Underwriting uses a checklist so that the same documents are requested for every file of the same type. A purchase needs the signed purchase agreement, proof of the down payment and its source, the income documents and the full appraisal. A refinance needs the income documents, the full appraisal, the payout statement for the existing mortgage and proof of the purpose of the new money. A rental property under RNT-4 needs the signed leases and the documents in POL-002 for rental income. A self-employed borrower needs the documents in POL-008 for the last 2 years. A home equity line of credit under HEL-1 needs the same income and appraisal documents as a mortgage. Every checklist includes proof of home insurance. The Underwriter ticks each item in the loan system as it is received.

## Conditions that are not met

If a condition is not met by the due date, the file owner must tell the broker or branch which condition is open and what will happen next. The file stays approved with conditions until the condition is met, the approval lapses or the closing date passes. Underwriting may not waive a condition. A condition that cannot be met may be changed only by a new decision of the role that approved the file, and if the change would break a policy rule it needs an exception under POL-010 on an EXC-100. If a condition remains open at closing, Mortgage Operations may not release funds (see PRC-006). The file owner records every change to a condition, the reason and the role that decided it.
