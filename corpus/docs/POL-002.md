# Income Verification

This policy sets the documents Underwriting must collect to verify income. Salaried borrowers need a recent pay stub and an employment letter dated within 30 days. Variable income is a 2-year average, and 50% of gross rental income is counted.

## Documents required for salaried income

Underwriting must verify the income of a salaried borrower with two documents: a recent pay stub and an employment letter. The pay stub must show the pay period, the year-to-date earnings and the employer's name. The employment letter must come from the employer, state the borrower's position, employment status, start date and base pay, and be dated within 30 days of the credit decision. The pay stub and the employment letter must agree on base pay. If they differ, the Underwriter must resolve the difference with the borrower before deciding and must note the explanation in the file. Income that is only stated by the borrower, with no document behind it, may not be counted. A salaried borrower with a second salaried job must supply the same two documents for each job, and Underwriting counts each job separately. The ratios that use the verified income are set in POL-003.

## How old an employment letter may be

An employment letter used to verify income must be dated within 30 days of the credit decision. The date on the letter is the date the employer signed it, not the date the broker or branch received it. If the letter is older than 30 days when the Underwriter reviews the file, the Underwriter must ask for a new letter and may not decide the file on the old one. A letter that is undated or unsigned is treated as missing. The Underwriter may accept a letter sent directly by the employer, or a letter supplied by the borrower that the Underwriter has confirmed with the employer. The Underwriter records how the letter was confirmed. The 30-day limit applies to the employment letter only; the pay stub must simply be recent enough to show the current pay period. Underwriting may not extend the 30-day limit without an approved exception under POL-010.

## Variable income: the 2-year average

Variable income is pay that changes from one period to the next, such as commission, bonus and overtime. Underwriting must count variable income as the average of the borrower's last 2 years of that income. The borrower must supply the income tax slips and the notices of assessment for each of the 2 years, and the Underwriter must check that they agree with the pay stub. If the borrower has less than 2 years of history for a type of variable income, Underwriting may not count that income and may count base pay only. If the most recent year is lower than the 2-year average, the Underwriter must use the lower figure. Variable income is added to base pay to give the income used in the ratios in POL-003. Self-employed borrowers are not covered here; their income is verified under POL-008.

## Rental income: 50% counted

Rental income is income the borrower receives from renting out part or all of a property, such as a suite in a 1-4 unit home or a separate rental property. Underwriting must count 50% of gross rents as income, and no more. The remaining 50% is treated as covering vacancy and the costs of running the rental, and it is not used anywhere else in the ratio calculation. The borrower must supply a signed lease or rental agreement for each rental unit. For a rental unit the borrower already owns, the Underwriter must also review the rental figures on the borrower's most recent income tax filing. A rental unit that is vacant at the time of the decision may be counted only if there is a signed lease that starts before closing. A purchase of a non-owner-occupied rental property is lent under RNT-4 (see PRD-006).

## Recording verified income in the file

For every borrower, the Underwriter must record in the file each income document received, the amount counted, the method used and the date of the check. Documents must be complete and legible. A page that is cut off, altered or missing is treated as not received. Income that cannot be verified under POL-002 is not counted, and the Underwriter must tell the broker or branch plainly which document is still needed. If a document looks altered or inconsistent with other documents in the file, the Underwriter must stop the file and raise a fraud flag instead of asking the borrower to resubmit. Verified income feeds the debt service ratios in POL-003 and the exceptions in POL-010. A request to accept different income documents is an exception request and must follow POL-010.
