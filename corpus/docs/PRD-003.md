# 5-Year Variable Closed (VR5-C)

PRD-003 describes the VR5-C mortgage: a 5-year closed variable-rate term priced at DMC Prime minus 0.55. Payments adjust when prime changes, the borrower may convert to a fixed term, and the penalty is 3 months' interest.

## VR5-C product summary

The VR5-C mortgage is the Demo Mortgage Co. 5-year variable closed mortgage, and its product code is VR5-C. The term is 5 years, the interest rate moves with DMC Prime, and the mortgage is closed, which means the borrower may prepay only as POL-009 allows. VR5-C is for an owner-occupied residential property that meets POL-006. A non-owner-occupied property is lent on under RNT-4 and not under VR5-C. The amortization may be up to 25 years for an insured mortgage and up to 30 years for an uninsured mortgage (see POL-007). The loan-to-value limit is the conventional limit in POL-007, and a loan above that limit needs mortgage default insurance. Every VR5-C application is tested at the qualifying rate under POL-004 and against the ratio limits in POL-003. An exception to any of these tests may be considered only as POL-010 allows.

## VR5-C rate and qualifying rate

The VR5-C contract rate is DMC Prime minus 0.55 points. On the rate sheet effective 2026-11-01, DMC Prime is 4.70%, so the VR5-C contract rate is 4.15% (see RATE-001). The rate changes whenever DMC Prime changes, and the borrower pays the new rate from the date of the change. Without a rate hold, the rate is the rate on the rate sheet in force on the funding date. The qualifying rate for VR5-C is the greater of the contract rate plus 1.75 points or 5.40%. At a contract rate of 4.15% the qualifying rate is 5.90%, because 4.15% plus 1.75 points is more than the 5.40% floor. The qualifying rate is a testing rate and is not the rate the borrower pays. Variable-rate interest on VR5-C is compounded monthly, as set out in POL-012. A rate hold fixes the spread and the starting rate for the hold period only (see RATE-003).

## VR5-C payments when prime changes

The regular payment on a VR5-C mortgage is recalculated whenever DMC Prime changes. Mortgage Operations applies the payment formula in POL-012 to the outstanding balance, the new contract rate and the remaining amortization, using monthly compounding. The new payment takes effect from the next payment date after the change, and Mortgage Operations tells the borrower the new payment in writing. The amortization period does not change because of a change in the rate. If DMC Prime falls, the VR5-C payment falls, and if DMC Prime rises, the VR5-C payment rises. Staff must not promise that the VR5-C payment will stay the same for the term, and must not forecast what DMC Prime will do. A change of payment amount made by Mortgage Operations because of a prime change carries no payment change fee.

## VR5-C prepayment privileges and penalty

The VR5-C mortgage has the same two prepayment privileges as the closed fixed products, as set by POL-009. The borrower may prepay up to 18% of the original principal in each anniversary year, and may raise the regular payment by up to 18% of the current regular payment, in both cases without penalty. Unused privileges do not carry forward. A prepayment on VR5-C that is above the privileges is subject to a penalty of 3 months' interest. Three months' interest is the interest on the amount prepaid for 3 months at the contract rate on the date of the prepayment. There is no interest rate differential charge on VR5-C. There is no penalty on the payout of VR5-C at its maturity date, and the discharge fee in RATE-002 is charged separately.

## VR5-C conversion to a fixed term

A borrower with a VR5-C mortgage may ask to convert to a fixed term during the term. The new fixed term must be at least as long as the remaining term of the VR5-C mortgage, so a borrower with more than 3 years remaining may convert only to a 5-year term, and a borrower with 3 years or less remaining may convert to a 3-year or a 5-year term. The fixed rate is the rate for the chosen product on the rate sheet in force on the date of the conversion (see RATE-001). No fee is charged for the conversion, because RATE-002 lists none. The converted mortgage follows the privileges and penalty of the new fixed product from the conversion date (see POL-009). Conversion is not requalified, because the borrower and the balance do not change.
