# Customer Communication Standards

PRC-009 sets the standards for every message staff send to borrowers and brokers: never promise approval or a rate beyond a rate hold, cite the policy ID, use plain language, and have a human review every draft before it is sent.

## What staff may not promise

Staff must never promise that a file will be approved. An approval is a decision recorded by the role with authority under PRC-004, and until it is recorded nothing is certain. Staff must never promise that an exception will be granted, because an exception request stays pending until a human approves it (see PRC-005). Staff must never promise a closing date, a renewal outcome or a refund. Staff may say what the process is, what has been received, what is still needed and what the next step will be. A message may say that a file is under review, that an exception review is under way, or that a document is outstanding. It may not say or suggest that the result is certain, likely or already decided. If a customer asks for a promise, the staff member must explain that the decision belongs to the role with authority and that the message cannot guarantee it.

## Rates and rate holds in messages

Staff must never promise a rate beyond a rate hold. A rate quoted in a message must be taken from the current rate sheet (see RATE-001) and must be described as the rate in force on the date of the message, which may change. The only rate that Demo Mortgage Co. guarantees is a rate covered by a rate hold, for the period set in RATE-003. A rate hold is not an approval, and a message that mentions a rate hold must say so. Staff must not describe the qualifying rate as the rate of the mortgage, because it is a testing rate only (see POL-004). Staff must not compare Demo Mortgage Co. rates with those of any other lender. If the rate sheet has changed since the customer was last given a rate, the message must say so and give the current figure.

## Citing the policy ID

When a message explains a decision, a requirement or a refusal, the sender must cite the ID of the policy that applies, for example POL-003 for a ratio limit or POL-005 for a down payment source. The ID lets the customer or broker ask the right question and lets the staff member check the current version. The sender must check that the cited document is current and not a superseded version that ends in -V1. A message must not quote a limit from memory. If no document supports the answer, the sender must say that no supporting document was found and must refer the question to the owning department. The sender must not invent a policy, and must not describe a policy as stricter or looser than it is to make a message easier to write.

## Plain language

Messages to borrowers and brokers must be written in plain language. The sender should use short sentences, say what the reader has to do and by when, and avoid internal terms. Where a term such as GDS, TDS or IRD is needed, the message must explain it in a few words the first time it appears. A message about a condition must name the document needed and the date it is due (see PRC-003). A message about a decline must give the reason in words the borrower can follow, cite the policy ID and say what the borrower may do next. The sender must not use pressure, such as saying that a rate will disappear, and must not use humour or comments about the customer's personal situation. Each message must refer to the file by loan number.

## Human review of every draft

Every draft message must be reviewed by a human before it is sent. This applies to messages written by a staff member and to drafts prepared with the help of a tool, including the policy assistant described in FAQ-006. The reviewer reads the draft in full and checks that it makes no promise, that any rate comes from RATE-001 or a rate hold, that it cites the right policy ID and that it contains no customer data beyond what is needed (see POL-011). The reviewer must be a person other than the tool that prepared the draft, and for a decline or a complaint response the reviewer must be a person with authority for the decision. A draft that has not been reviewed must not be sent. The sender keeps a copy of the reviewed message in the loan file.
