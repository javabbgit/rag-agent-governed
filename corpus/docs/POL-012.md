# Payment Calculation Method

POL-012 sets how Mortgage Operations calculates mortgage payments. It defines the amortization formula, semi-annual compounding for fixed rates and monthly compounding for variable rates, the payment frequencies including accelerated ones, and rounding to the cent.

## The amortization payment formula

Mortgage Operations must calculate the regular payment of a Demo Mortgage Co. mortgage with the amortization formula: payment = P x i / (1 - (1 + i)^(-n)). In the formula P is the loan amount, i is the interest rate for one payment period written as a decimal, and n is the total number of payments over the amortization period. The result is the level payment that repays the loan in full by the end of the amortization period. The amortization period is limited by POL-007. The same formula is used for the payment shown to the borrower and for the payment used in the debt service ratios of POL-003, except that the ratios use the qualifying rate from POL-004 in effect of the contract rate. Staff may not use a different formula, a rounded interest rate or a spreadsheet shortcut that gives a different result.

## Compounding: fixed semi-annual, variable monthly

The rate for one payment period depends on how the interest rate is compounded. For a fixed-rate mortgage, the stated annual rate is compounded semi-annually. Mortgage Operations converts it to a payment-period rate with the formula i = (1 + r / 2)^(2 / f) - 1, where r is the stated annual rate as a decimal and f is the number of payments per year. For a variable-rate mortgage, the stated annual rate is compounded monthly, and the payment-period rate for monthly payments is i = r / 12. For variable-rate payments made more often than monthly, the monthly rate is converted to the payment period in the same way. The compounding rule applies to FX5-C, FX3-C, SEA-5, RNT-4 and FX1-O as fixed-rate products, and to VR5-C as a variable-rate product. The home equity line of credit HEL-1 calculates interest as stated in PRD-005.

## Payment frequencies

The borrower may choose among these payment frequencies: monthly with 12 payments per year, semi-monthly with 24, bi-weekly with 26, weekly with 52, accelerated bi-weekly with 26 and accelerated weekly with 52. For the standard frequencies, Mortgage Operations applies the formula in POL-012 with f set to the number of payments per year. For an accelerated frequency, the payment is based on the monthly payment: accelerated bi-weekly is half of the monthly payment, and accelerated weekly is one quarter of the monthly payment. Because there are more payment periods in a year than in the standard schedule, the accelerated frequencies repay the loan faster than the matching standard frequencies. A change of payment frequency after closing carries the fee shown in RATE-002 and takes effect from the next payment date.

## Rounding rules

Every payment must be rounded to the nearest cent, with a half cent rounded up. Mortgage Operations rounds the regular payment once, after the formula has been applied, and does not round the interest rate or any figure in the middle of the calculation. The accelerated payments are calculated from the rounded monthly payment, and each accelerated payment is then rounded to the cent. Interest charged on each payment date is rounded to the cent. The final payment of the term or of the amortization is adjusted so that the loan balance is exactly zero, so it may differ from the regular payment by a few cents. Rounding differences are never shown to the borrower as fees and are never charged as an extra amount.

## Variable-rate payments when prime changes

The payment on a variable-rate mortgage such as VR5-C is recalculated whenever DMC Prime changes, because the contract rate is DMC Prime plus or minus the spread of the product. Mortgage Operations applies the formula in POL-012 to the outstanding balance, the new contract rate and the remaining amortization, using monthly compounding. The new payment takes effect from the next payment date after the change, and Mortgage Operations tells the borrower the new payment amount in writing. The amortization period stays the same unless the borrower asks for a change and Underwriting agrees. The payment never changes because of the qualifying rate, which is used only to test an application under POL-004. A borrower who asks why the payment changed should be told that DMC Prime changed, and should be given the date of the change, the new contract rate and the new payment. Staff may not promise that the payment will stay the same for the term.

## Example: monthly payment on an FX5-C mortgage

A borrower has an FX5-C mortgage of 400,000 at a contract rate of 4.69%, amortized over 25 years with monthly payments. The fixed rate is compounded semi-annually, so the monthly rate is i = (1 + 0.0469 / 2)^(2 / 12) - 1 = 0.003871. The number of payments is n = 300. The payment is 400,000 x 0.003871 / (1 - (1.003871)^(-300)) = 2,256.34, rounded to the cent. The accelerated bi-weekly payment is 2,256.34 / 2 = 1,128.17 and the accelerated weekly payment is 2,256.34 / 4 = 564.09. For a VR5-C mortgage of 400,000 at 4.15%, the monthly rate is 0.0415 / 12 = 0.003458, and the payment over 25 years is 2,144.62.
