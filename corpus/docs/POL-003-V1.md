# Debt Service Ratio Limits (GDS/TDS)

Superseded on 2026-09-01 by POL-003 version 2.0. POL-003-V1 set the maximum gross debt service (GDS) ratio at 33% and the maximum total debt service (TDS) ratio at 40%, with both ratios based on the qualifying rate from POL-004.

## GDS and TDS limits

The gross debt service ratio (GDS) may not exceed 33%. The total debt service ratio (TDS) may not exceed 40%. Both limits apply to every application, and both must be met. GDS measures the share of gross monthly income that goes to housing costs. TDS measures the share of gross monthly income that goes to housing costs plus all other monthly debt payments. A file that is within the GDS limit but over the TDS limit does not meet POL-003-V1, and neither does a file that is within TDS but over GDS. The Underwriter calculates both ratios on every file, using the income verified under POL-002 and the qualifying rate set in POL-004. The limits in POL-003-V1 apply to files decided before 2026-09-01.

## What counts in the GDS ratio

The GDS ratio counts the monthly housing costs of the property being financed. Underwriting must include the mortgage payment calculated at the qualifying rate (see POL-004), the property tax, and heating costs. Heating is counted at 100 per month for every property, whatever the actual bill, so that files are treated the same way. For a condominium, Underwriting must also count 50% of the monthly condo fee, and the other 50% is left out. The total of these amounts is divided by gross monthly income, which is income before tax and deductions, to give the GDS ratio. A line of credit secured on the property is included at its monthly payment calculated under POL-004. Rental income counted under POL-002 is part of gross monthly income. It is not subtracted from housing costs.

## What counts in the TDS ratio

The TDS ratio counts everything in the GDS ratio plus the other monthly debt payments of every borrower on the file. Underwriting must include the minimum monthly payment on each credit card and line of credit, the monthly payment on each loan, any support payments the borrower is required to make, and the payment on any other mortgage. Each payment comes from the credit bureau report or from a statement the borrower supplies. A debt that will be paid off from the proceeds of the mortgage, and that is shown as paid in the instructions to the lawyer, is left out. A debt that the borrower says is paid but that still appears on the credit bureau report must be counted until proof of payment is in the file. The total is divided by gross monthly income to give the TDS ratio.

## Why both ratios use the qualifying rate

GDS and TDS must be calculated with the qualifying rate defined in POL-004, not with the contract rate the borrower will actually pay. The qualifying rate is the greater of the contract rate plus 1.75 points or 5.40%. Using it tests whether the borrower could still afford the mortgage if payments rose. The mortgage payment in both ratios is calculated on the full loan amount, using the amortization allowed for the file and the payment method in POL-012. Underwriting may not use a lower rate, a longer amortization or a smaller loan amount to bring a ratio under the limit. If the calculation changes because the loan amount or the contract rate changes before closing, the Underwriter must recalculate both ratios.

## Example: GDS and TDS for a sample condominium file

A borrower has gross monthly income of 9,000. The mortgage payment at the qualifying rate is 2,300, the property tax is 350 per month, heating is counted at 100, and the condo fee is 400 per month, of which 50% is counted, or 200. Housing costs are 2,300 + 350 + 100 + 200 = 2,950. GDS is 2,950 / 9,000 = 32.8%, which is within the 33% limit. The borrower also pays 500 per month on a personal loan and a credit card. TDS is (2,950 + 500) / 9,000 = 38.3%, which is within the 40% limit. This hypothetical file meets both limits. If the condo fee were 800, housing costs would be 3,150, GDS would be 35.0%, and the file would be over the GDS limit.

## What happens when a ratio is over the limit

An Underwriter may not approve a file whose GDS or TDS ratio is over the limit in POL-003-V1. The Underwriter may approve it only after an exception has been requested and approved under POL-010. The exception request is raised on form EXC-100 and is routed to the role that has authority for the size of the overage (see PRC-005). The request must show both ratios, the compensating factors and the justification. A ratio is not rounded down to bring it under the limit; any ratio above the limit, even by a small amount, is over the limit. Staff may not tell a borrower or broker that a file with a ratio over the limit will be approved. They may say that the file needs an exception review and that the review is not a promise of approval.
