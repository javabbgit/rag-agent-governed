# Credit Policy Overview

This policy states what the Demo Mortgage Co. credit policies cover, how policies, processes and FAQs rank against each other, which departments own them, and why every exception to policy must go through POL-010.

## Scope of the credit policy

The Demo Mortgage Co. credit policy applies to every residential mortgage and home equity line of credit that the company originates, whether the application arrives through a mortgage broker or through a branch. It covers the whole life of a credit decision: income verification, debt service limits, the qualifying rate, down payment, loan-to-value limits, property eligibility and the approval authority of each role. It also covers renewals, because a renewal is a credit decision even when the borrower is not requalified. The credit policy binds Underwriting, Mortgage Operations, Broker Channel and Customer Care staff who touch a file, and Credit Risk, which writes most of it. It does not cover marketing, staff administration or technology operations, which are governed elsewhere. A staff member who is unsure whether a rule applies to a file must ask the owning department before deciding, and may not rely on memory of how an earlier file was handled. Individual policies are numbered POL-001 and up, and each one states one decision area.

## Document hierarchy: policy, process and FAQ

Demo Mortgage Co. keeps three kinds of credit documents, ranked in this order: policy, then process, then FAQ. A policy (POL) states what must be true before the company lends, for example a ratio limit or a document requirement. A process (PRC) states who does what, in which order and by when, in order to carry out a policy. An FAQ answers common questions in plain words for borrowers, brokers or staff, and it never adds a rule. When two documents disagree, the higher-ranked document wins: policy over process, and process over FAQ. A staff member who finds a disagreement must report it to the owning department so that the lower-ranked document is corrected. Product documents (PRD) and rate documents (RATE) supply product terms, rates and fees; they apply the policy and may not relax it. Every numeric limit used in a process, product or FAQ must match the policy that sets it, and the document must cite that policy by ID.

## Who owns each policy

Every policy has one owning department, named in the owner field of its front matter. Owners are departments, never individuals, so ownership does not change when people change roles. Credit Risk owns the credit limits: the ratio, qualifying rate, down payment and exception policies. Underwriting owns the income verification and property eligibility policies. Compliance owns the customer data privacy policy. Product owns the product and rate documents. The owning department answers questions about its policy, reviews it on a regular schedule and approves every change to it. A change takes effect on the effective date shown in the document, and the previous version is kept as a superseded document. A staff member who wants a rule changed must send the request to the owning department. Staff may not change how they apply a rule on their own, even when the rule seems out of date, because the credit policy only works when every file is judged the same way.

## How exceptions to credit policy are handled

A credit policy rule may be set aside only through the exception process. Every exception to credit policy goes through POL-010, which lists the items that can be excepted, the roles that can approve each one and the compensating factors that are needed. Staff may not waive a rule informally, may not give a verbal approval, and may not promise a borrower or broker that an exception will be granted. The request is raised on form EXC-100 and follows the procedure in PRC-005. It stays pending until a human with the right authority approves it. Some rules can never be excepted, including privacy rules and fraud flags (see POL-010). An approved exception applies only to the file it was approved for and does not become a precedent for later files. The Underwriter records the approved exception in the file so that the decision can be audited. The other credit policies state their own limits and leave the exception authority levels to POL-010.

## Policy versions and superseded documents

Each policy carries a version, an effective date and a status. The status is either current or superseded. A current document is the rule that applies today. When a policy changes, the new version is issued under the same ID with a higher version number, and the earlier version is kept as a separate document whose ID ends in -V1. A superseded document begins with a sentence naming the date and the document that replaced it. Staff may not use a superseded document to decide a new file. It is kept only so that older decisions can be understood and audited. If a borrower or broker quotes an older limit, the staff member must check the current version before answering, and must cite the policy ID in the reply. A document that relies on another policy must cite its ID, for example POL-004, so that readers can find the current version.
