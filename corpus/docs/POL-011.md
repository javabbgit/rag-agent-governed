# Customer Data Privacy and Handling

POL-011 sets how staff handle customer data. Staff must never type a SIN, card number or bank account number into a chat tool, must refer to customers by loan number only in chat tools, and may see only the data their role needs.

## Data that may never be typed into a chat tool

Staff must never type or paste a customer's social insurance number (SIN), payment card number or bank account number into any chat tool. A chat tool is any program in which a person types a message and gets a reply, including the policy assistant described in FAQ-006, an instant messaging program and any other assistant. Staff must also never type a customer's date of birth, home address, telephone number, email address or employer details into a chat tool. The rule applies even when the customer asks for help in that way, and it applies to messages that look anonymous. It also applies to photographs or copies of documents that show these details. If a staff member sees that such data has been typed into a chat tool, by themselves or by anyone else, they must report it to Compliance on the same business day. Privacy rules are never exceptable under POL-010.

## Referring to customers by loan number only

When staff need to talk about a customer in a chat tool, they must refer to the customer by loan number only. The loan number has the format DMC-YYYY-NNNNN, where YYYY is the year the file was opened and NNNNN is a five-digit sequence number. For example, the form DMC-2026-00123 identifies a file. A loan number identifies a file inside Demo Mortgage Co. and does not reveal the customer to anyone outside the company. Staff must not add the customer's name, a nickname, an employer or the street of the property next to the loan number. Questions to a chat tool should describe the situation in general terms, for example the product, the ratios and the policy involved, and not the personal story of the borrower. The same loan-number rule applies in written notes that are shared across departments.

## Least-privilege access by role

Each role may see only the customer data needed for its work. This is called least-privilege access. A Customer Service Representative may view the status of a file and the contact details needed to answer a customer, but not the full financial documents. A Loan Officer may view the application and the supporting documents of the files they handle. An Underwriter and a Senior Underwriter may view the whole file of the files assigned to them. The Credit Committee may view the file and the exception request put before it. Access is granted by the department that manages the system, on the request of a manager, and is removed when the role changes. A staff member may not look at a file out of curiosity or because they know the customer. The access rights for each role are set out in PRC-004.

## Storing and sharing customer documents

Customer documents must be kept only in the system of record for mortgage files. Staff may not keep copies in personal folders, on removable drives or in personal email. A document may be shared inside Demo Mortgage Co. only with a person whose role needs it, and only through the system of record or an approved internal channel. A document may be sent outside the company only to the borrower, the borrower's broker or the borrower's lawyer, through an approved channel, and only for the file concerned. Staff must not photograph screens or print customer documents unless the process in the PRC documents requires it, and printed copies must be destroyed securely after use. Anything that has been stored or sent in the wrong place must be reported to Compliance on the same business day.

## Reporting a privacy incident

A privacy incident is any case where customer data is seen, typed, stored or sent in a way that POL-011 does not allow. Examples are a SIN typed into a chat tool, a document sent to the wrong person or a file opened without a work reason. A staff member who causes or sees an incident must stop the activity and report it to Compliance on the same business day, giving the loan number and a short description but not the customer data itself. Compliance records the incident, decides whether the customer or others must be told and tells the owning department what to fix. Reporting an incident in good time is never treated as a reason for blame. Failing to report one is a breach of the rule. Privacy rules may never be excepted (see POL-010).
