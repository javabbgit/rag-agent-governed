# Down Payment Requirements and Sources

This policy sets the minimum down payment: 6% of the first 400,000 of the price plus 12% of the rest, and 20% when the price is 1,200,000 or more. It also sets which sources are accepted, including a 90-day savings history, and rejects a borrowed down payment.

## Minimum down payment by purchase price

For a purchase below 1,200,000, the minimum down payment is 6% of the first 400,000 of the purchase price plus 12% of the portion of the price above 400,000. Underwriting must calculate the minimum from the purchase price, not from the loan amount the borrower asks for. A borrower may put down more than the minimum. If the down payment is less than 20% of the price, the mortgage needs mortgage default insurance (see POL-007). The minimum is a floor for the amount, and the source of the money must also meet the source rules in POL-005. A file where the amount is correct but the source is not accepted does not meet POL-005. The Underwriter records the purchase price, the minimum down payment, the amount the borrower is paying and the source of each part in the file.

## Minimum down payment at a price of 1,200,000 or more

When the purchase price is 1,200,000 or more, the minimum down payment is 20% of the whole purchase price. The tiered 6% and 12% calculation does not apply at that price level. A purchase at exactly 1,200,000 needs 20% down. The 20% rule applies to the price in the purchase agreement, and the Underwriter may not lower the price used by treating part of it as a separate item. With 20% down the loan-to-value ratio is at most 80%, so mortgage default insurance is not needed (see POL-007), and the borrower must have the full 20% from accepted sources. Staff must tell the broker or borrower at the start of the file that this higher minimum applies, so that the borrower does not arrange a smaller down payment based on the tiered calculation.

## Example: minimum down payment on a 600,000 purchase

A borrower is buying a home for 600,000. The first 400,000 of the price needs 6%, which is 24,000. The remaining 200,000 needs 12%, which is 24,000. The minimum down payment is 24,000 + 24,000 = 48,000, which is 8% of the price. The borrower's loan amount cannot be more than 552,000. Now take a purchase of 1,200,000. The price is at the high-price threshold, so the minimum is 20% of the whole price, which is 240,000, and the tiered calculation is not used. For a purchase of 1,000,000, the minimum is 6% of 400,000 plus 12% of 600,000, which is 24,000 + 72,000 = 96,000. In each case the Underwriter compares the minimum with the money the borrower can show from accepted sources and records both amounts in the file.

## Own savings need a 90-day history

When the borrower pays the down payment from their own savings, the savings must have a 90-day history. Underwriting must see statements for the account or accounts that cover the last 90 days and show that the money has been in the account for that time. A single large deposit that appears inside the 90 days must be explained and documented. Acceptable explanations include the sale of a property, a bonus shown on a pay stub, or a gift that meets the rules below. If a deposit cannot be traced to an acceptable source, the Underwriter may not count it toward the down payment. Money from the sale of the borrower's existing home does not need a 90-day history, but the Underwriter must see the signed sale agreement and the lawyer's statement of the sale proceeds.

## Gifted down payment and the gift letter

A down payment may include a gift from a close family member. Every gift needs a signed gift letter. The gift letter must name the donor and their relationship to the borrower, state the amount, state that the money is a gift, and state that no repayment is expected in any form. The donor must sign it. The Underwriter must also see proof that the money has moved from the donor's account to the borrower's account or to the lawyer's trust account before closing. A gift that has no signed letter is not accepted. A letter that says the money may be repaid, or that is signed by someone other than the donor, is not accepted. Underwriting records the gift amount and the date the funds arrived in the file.

## A borrowed down payment is not accepted

Demo Mortgage Co. does not accept a borrowed down payment. The down payment may not come from a personal loan, a line of credit, a credit card cash advance or any other borrowing, whether the borrowing is secured or unsecured. The ban applies even when the borrower says they will repay the money quickly. If a bank statement shows a recent deposit that looks like loan proceeds, the Underwriter must ask for proof of where it came from. If the deposit is borrowed money, it may not be counted. A request to accept a borrowed down payment is not an item that can be excepted under POL-010. The Underwriter must tell the broker or branch that the file cannot proceed on that basis and may suggest a lower purchase price or a larger down payment from accepted sources.
