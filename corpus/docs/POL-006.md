# Property Eligibility

This policy lists the properties Demo Mortgage Co. will lend on: 1-4 unit residential homes and condos in Canada. It names the ineligible property types, limits non-owner-occupied lending to RNT-4, and requires a full appraisal on every file.

## Eligible property types

Demo Mortgage Co. lends only on residential properties located in Canada. An eligible property is a 1-4 unit residential home or a condominium unit. A one-unit property may be a detached, semi-detached or row home. A two- to four-unit property is eligible when it is a single legal property used for housing. A condominium unit is eligible when it is a residential unit in a registered condominium. The property must have a clear legal description, legal access from a public road and year-round occupancy as a residence. The Underwriter checks the property type against the appraisal and the purchase agreement, and must record the property type in the file. If the documents describe the property differently, the Underwriter must resolve the difference before deciding the file. Property types that are not listed here are not eligible, and the Underwriter may not decide them on judgment alone.

## Ineligible property types

Demo Mortgage Co. may not lend on vacant land. It may not lend on a mobile home that does not sit on a permanent foundation. A mobile home that is fixed to a permanent foundation and is legally part of the real property may be assessed as a one-unit home, and the appraisal must confirm the foundation. A property with more than four units is not eligible. A property that cannot be lived in as a residence, or that is not connected to year-round services, is not eligible. If any part of the property is not used for housing, the Underwriter must refer the file to Credit Risk before going further. Property eligibility is not an item that can be excepted under POL-010, so a file on an ineligible property must be declined. The Underwriter explains to the broker or branch that the property type does not meet POL-006.

## Owner-occupied and non-owner-occupied properties

An eligible property is normally the home where the borrower will live. This is called owner-occupied. A property the borrower will not live in is non-owner-occupied, and Demo Mortgage Co. lends on it only under RNT-4 (see PRD-006). The Underwriter must confirm at application whether the borrower will live in the property and must record the answer in the file. If the borrower says the property is owner-occupied but the documents show that it is to be rented out, the Underwriter must stop and clarify before going further. A borrower may rent out part of an owner-occupied 1-4 unit property, such as a suite, and the rental income is counted under POL-002. The occupancy stated at application applies for the whole file. If the occupancy changes before closing, the Underwriter must review the file again and decide which product applies.

## Full appraisal requirement

Every file needs a full appraisal of the property. An appraisal is a written report by a qualified appraiser who has inspected the property and states its market value. Demo Mortgage Co. does not lend on an automated estimate of value or on the purchase price alone. Mortgage Operations orders the appraisal from an approved appraiser. The appraisal must describe the property type, the number of units, the condition and the legal description. The Underwriter must read the full appraisal and must check that it supports the property types in POL-006. For loan-to-value limits, the value used is the lower of the purchase price and the appraised value (see POL-007). If the appraised value is lower than the purchase price, the borrower must make up the difference with extra down payment from accepted sources under POL-005.
