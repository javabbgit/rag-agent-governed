# Borrower FAQ: Paying Off Your Mortgage Faster

Answers to common questions about paying extra, paying off your mortgage early and the penalty. You can pay up to 18% extra each year on a closed mortgage, and the penalty depends on whether your rate is fixed or variable.

## Can I pay extra on my mortgage?

Yes. A closed Demo Mortgage Co. mortgage has two extra-payment privileges that you can use each year without a penalty (see POL-009). The first is a lump-sum payment, where you pay a one-time amount on top of your regular payments. The second is a payment increase, where you raise your regular payment. You can use both in the same year. These privileges apply to FX5-C, FX3-C, VR5-C, SEA-5 and RNT-4. They do not apply to the open mortgage FX1-O, because FX1-O has no penalty at all, or to the home equity line of credit HEL-1, which has its own terms. To pay extra, ask Mortgage Operations how to make the payment, and ask for the amount you have left for the year.

## How much extra can I pay each year without a penalty?

On a closed Demo Mortgage Co. mortgage, you can pay up to 18% of the original principal as lump sums in each anniversary year, without a penalty (see POL-009). Your anniversary year runs from one anniversary of your closing date to the next. The limit is based on what you first borrowed, not on what you owe today. For example, if you first borrowed 400,000, you can pay up to 72,000 in lump sums each anniversary year. You can also raise your regular payment by up to 18% of your current regular payment, once in each anniversary year. If your regular payment is 2,000, you can raise it by up to 360, to 2,360. The two limits are separate, so you can use both in the same year. Mortgage Operations keeps track of what you have paid.

## Can I pay off my mortgage early?

Yes, but a penalty can apply. If you pay off a closed mortgage in full before it matures, or pay more than your privileges allow, a prepayment penalty is charged on the amount above your privileges (see POL-009). If you pay off your mortgage at its maturity date, there is no penalty. A discharge fee of $295 is charged when your mortgage is paid out and discharged, in addition to any penalty (see RATE-002). If you have the open mortgage FX1-O, you can pay off any amount at any time without a penalty. If you are thinking of paying off or refinancing a closed mortgage before it matures, ask Mortgage Operations for a payout statement, which shows the exact penalty before you decide.

## How is the penalty worked out?

On a fixed-rate closed mortgage, the penalty is the greater of 3 months' interest or the interest rate differential, which is called the IRD (see POL-009). Three months' interest is the interest on the amount you prepay for 3 months at your rate. The IRD is the difference between your rate and the rate Demo Mortgage Co. could charge today for a term equal to what you have left, applied to the amount you prepay for the time you have left. Mortgage Operations works out both and charges the greater one. On a variable-rate closed mortgage, VR5-C, the penalty is 3 months' interest, and there is no IRD charge. The fixed-rate products are FX5-C, FX3-C, SEA-5 and RNT-4. The penalty is shown on your payout statement.

## Is there a mortgage with no penalty?

Yes. The 1-year fixed open mortgage, FX1-O, lets you prepay any amount at any time without a penalty (see PRD-004). In exchange, the FX1-O rate is higher than the rate of the closed terms (see RATE-001). The home equity line of credit, HEL-1, also has no prepayment penalty, and you can repay any part of what you have drawn at any time (see PRD-005). The closed mortgages, FX5-C, FX3-C, VR5-C, SEA-5 and RNT-4, have the privileges and penalty described above. Staff cannot tell you which product is better for you, because that depends on your own plans, but they can explain how each one works, and they cite POL-009 and the product document when they do. If you want to compare products, you can also ask for the current rate sheet, RATE-001, which shows the rate of each one.

## Do unused extra payments carry over to next year?

No. If you do not use all of your extra-payment privileges in one anniversary year, the unused amount is lost at the end of that year and does not carry forward (see POL-009). Your limits start again on each anniversary of your closing date, and again on the first day of a renewed term. For example, if you pay only 45,000 in lump sums in a year when your limit is higher, the next year's limit is the same as it was before, not larger. The same applies to the payment increase. Mortgage Operations records your anniversary date and applies the new limits from that date. If you want to pay down your mortgage faster, plan your extra payments for each year.
