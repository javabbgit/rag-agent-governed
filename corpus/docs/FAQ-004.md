# Broker FAQ: Submissions and Conditions

Answers for mortgage brokers about submitting applications and meeting conditions: the broker portal, what makes an application complete, the acknowledgement within 1 business day, and conditions due 10 business days before closing.

## How do I submit a mortgage application?

You submit an application through the broker portal. A borrower may instead apply at a branch, where a Loan Officer enters the application into the same loan system, so both routes use the same form and the same checks (see PRC-001). Mortgage Operations does not accept applications by any other route, such as a message or a personal email. If you send documents or details outside the broker portal, you will be asked to submit them again through the portal. Broker Channel manages the relationship with brokers and can help you to submit, but it does not take applications. When you submit, quote the loan number in later messages about the file, and do not put the borrower's name in a chat tool (see POL-011).

## What does a complete application need?

An application is complete only when it holds everything on the checklist in PRC-001. For each borrower you need the full legal name, the contact details, the identification documents required by Compliance, the employment and income details with the documents required by POL-002 or POL-008, the assets and the source of the down payment, and the consent to a credit bureau check. For the property you need the address, the property type, the number of units, the occupancy and, for a purchase, the signed purchase agreement. For the loan you need the product, the term, the loan amount, the amortization and the payment frequency. You also need every debt the borrower holds. An application with any item missing is incomplete.

## How will I know you received the application?

Mortgage Operations acknowledges every application within 1 business day of receiving it (see PRC-001). The acknowledgement goes to you when you submitted through the broker portal. It confirms that the application was received, gives the loan number and says whether the application is complete or incomplete. If it is incomplete, it lists each missing item in plain language. The acknowledgement does not say that the application will be approved, does not quote a rate other than one covered by a rate hold, and does not give an approval date. A rate hold is not an approval (see RATE-003). The loan number has the format DMC-YYYY-NNNNN and stays the same for the whole life of the file.

## What happens to an incomplete application?

Mortgage Operations does not pass an incomplete application to Underwriting. It keeps the file in an incomplete status and sends you the list of missing items. When you supply them, Mortgage Operations checks the list again, and a complete file is released to Underwriting, which starts the workflow in PRC-002. You can ask about the status of a file and you will be told whether it is incomplete, with Underwriting or decided, but you will not be told what the decision will be before it is made. If you withdraw an application, tell Mortgage Operations, which records the withdrawal and closes the file under the same loan number. Staff may not tell you what the decision will be, and they will not describe a file as approved until Underwriting or the role with authority has recorded the decision in the loan system.

## Which conditions will I see on an approval?

When Underwriting approves a file with conditions, the conditions are the facts that must still be confirmed before the loan is funded (see PRC-003). The standard conditions are a satisfactory full appraisal, proof that the down payment comes from accepted sources and, for a gift, the signed gift letter, an employment letter dated within 30 days where income depends on it, confirmation from the mortgage default insurer where insurance is needed, proof of home insurance, payout statements and discharge of any debt or mortgage paid from the proceeds, and the lawyer's confirmation of clear title. Underwriting may add a condition specific to the file, and each condition names the document needed and who must supply it.

## When are conditions due and who signs them off?

Every condition must be met, and the supporting document must be in the loan system, at least 10 business days before the closing date (see PRC-003). The approval states the due date. The Underwriter signs off the appraisal, the down payment and gift letter, the income documents and the insurance confirmation. Mortgage Operations signs off the home insurance proof, the payout statements and discharges, and the lawyer's title confirmation. If a document arrives after the due date, it may still be accepted, but Mortgage Operations must be told at once because the closing may have to be moved. Conditions cannot be waived, and you should not tell the borrower that a late condition will not affect the closing.
