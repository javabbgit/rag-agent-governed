# Funding and Closing

PRC-006 sets how Mortgage Operations closes and funds an approved mortgage: the closing instructions to the borrower's lawyer, the final conditions check, the steps to release funds and the post-closing file audit.

## Closing instructions to the borrower's lawyer

Mortgage Operations sends closing instructions to the borrower's lawyer once the approval is in place and the conditions due before closing have been signed off (see PRC-003). The instructions state the loan number, the loan amount, the product and term, the contract rate, the payment amount and frequency, the closing date and the conditions the lawyer must satisfy. They also list the debts and mortgages that must be paid from the proceeds and ask the lawyer to confirm clear title and the registration of the mortgage. The instructions use the loan number to identify the file and do not include any more customer data than the lawyer needs. Mortgage Operations sends them only through the approved channel and only to the lawyer named on the file. If the lawyer asks for a change to the terms, Mortgage Operations refers the request to the Underwriter and does not change anything itself.

## Final conditions check

Before funds are released, Mortgage Operations performs a final conditions check on the file. The check confirms that every condition on the approval has been signed off by the right role (see PRC-003), that the approval has not lapsed, that the loan amount and terms in the closing instructions agree with the approval, and that any exception on the file has an approval recorded on the EXC-100 (see PRC-005). Mortgage Operations also checks that the home insurance proof is in the file and that the lawyer has confirmed the items asked for in the instructions. The person who performs the final check records the date and signs it in the loan system. If any item is open, the file is not funded, and Mortgage Operations tells the file owner and the broker or branch which item is open.

## Funds release steps

Mortgage Operations releases funds in a set order. First, a person other than the one who prepared the file confirms that the final conditions check is signed. Second, Mortgage Operations confirms the amount to be advanced, after any deduction for insurance premiums or fees that are payable from the proceeds. Third, it sends the funds to the lawyer's trust account on the closing date, in the amount stated in the closing instructions and no other. Fourth, it records the date and amount of the advance in the loan system and sets the first payment date. The person who releases the funds and the person who approved the file must be different people. Funds are never released on a verbal instruction, and a change to the amount or the account requires a new written instruction that is checked again.

## Post-closing file audit

After closing, Mortgage Operations audits every funded file. The audit checks that the file contains the signed approval, every condition and its sign-off, any EXC-100 with its approval, the closing instructions, the lawyer's confirmation of registration and the record of the advance. It also checks that the numbers on the approval agree with the figures in the loan system, including the contract rate, the amortization and the payment. Any missing document is requested at once from the person responsible. Mortgage Operations records the result of each audit and sends a summary to Credit Risk, which reviews the findings for patterns. A finding that suggests a policy was not followed is sent to the owning department. The audit result does not change the approval, but it may lead to a correction of the file.

## When closing is delayed

If the closing date moves, Mortgage Operations tells the lawyer, the broker or branch and the file owner on the same business day. The file owner checks whether the delay affects the approval, the rate hold or any condition that has a date. A rate hold ends at the date set by RATE-003, and a delay in closing does not extend it. A rate hold extension is an exception and must follow PRC-005. If the delay means that a document will be out of date, such as an employment letter that is older than 30 days at the revised closing, the Underwriter must ask for a new one before funding. Mortgage Operations records the new closing date and the reason for the delay.
