# Loan-to-Value and Default Insurance

POL-007 sets the conventional maximum loan-to-value ratio at 80%. A loan above 80% requires mortgage default insurance. It also sets the maximum amortization at 25 years for insured mortgages and 30 years for uninsured mortgages.

## Conventional loan-to-value limit

The conventional loan-to-value ratio (LTV) of a Demo Mortgage Co. mortgage may not exceed 80%. LTV is the loan amount divided by the property value. A mortgage at or below 80% LTV is called a conventional mortgage, and it does not need mortgage default insurance. Underwriting must calculate LTV on every file and record the loan amount, the property value used and the result in the file. The limit applies to the total of all mortgage debt secured on the property with Demo Mortgage Co. at the time of the decision. Product documents may set a lower maximum for a product, and the lower figure then applies (see the product limits below). A file whose LTV is over the applicable limit and is not insured does not meet POL-007. It may be approved only after an exception under POL-010, because LTV is an item that can be excepted.

## When mortgage default insurance is required

A mortgage with an LTV above 80% must be covered by mortgage default insurance before Demo Mortgage Co. funds it. Mortgage default insurance protects the lender, not the borrower, if the borrower fails to pay. The insurance is arranged with an approved mortgage default insurer, the premium is paid by the borrower, and the borrower may ask for the premium to be added to the loan. Underwriting must confirm that the insurer has approved the loan before the file is approved, and must keep the insurer's confirmation in the file. The loan must also meet the insurer's own rules, which may be stricter than the rules of Demo Mortgage Co. A down payment below 20% of the price, calculated under POL-005, leads to an LTV above 80%, so the borrower needs insurance in that case. If the insurer declines the loan, Underwriting may not approve the file as an insured mortgage.

## How loan-to-value is calculated

LTV is calculated as the loan amount divided by the lower of the purchase price and the appraised value. For a purchase, Underwriting must use the lower of the purchase price in the agreement and the value in the full appraisal required by POL-006. For a refinance or a renewal with new money, Underwriting uses the appraised value. The loan amount is the amount advanced to the borrower, including any insurance premium that has been added to the loan. For a home equity line of credit, the loan amount is the full credit limit and not the amount drawn. LTV is rounded to one decimal place, and a result above the limit is over the limit however small the excess. If the appraised value is lower than the purchase price, the borrower must either reduce the loan or increase the down payment from accepted sources under POL-005.

## Example: LTV on a purchase with a lower appraisal

A borrower agrees to buy a home for 500,000 and asks for a loan of 400,000. The full appraisal comes back at 490,000. The lower of the two values is 490,000, so Underwriting divides 400,000 by 490,000. LTV is 81.6%, which is above 80%, so the loan needs mortgage default insurance. The borrower has two choices. The borrower may keep the loan at 400,000 and arrange insurance, or may reduce the loan to 392,000, which is 80.0% of 490,000, and bring the extra 8,000 as additional down payment. With a loan of 392,000 the mortgage is conventional and no insurance is needed. If the borrower keeps the loan at 400,000 and the insurer approves it, the file may go forward as an insured mortgage with the amortization limit for insured mortgages.

## Maximum amortization: 25 years insured, 30 years uninsured

The amortization period is the number of years over which the payments would repay the loan in full. The maximum amortization is 25 years for an insured mortgage and 30 years for an uninsured mortgage. An insured mortgage is one that is covered by mortgage default insurance. An uninsured mortgage is a conventional mortgage with no insurance. Underwriting may not approve a longer amortization than the limit that applies to the file, and may not extend the amortization to bring a debt service ratio under the limit in POL-003. The amortization is stated in the mortgage terms and is used to calculate the payment under POL-012. On a straight renewal, the remaining amortization may not be extended beyond the original limit. A borrower who asks for a longer amortization must be told that the request cannot be approved.

## Loan-to-value limits set by product

Some products have a lower maximum LTV than the conventional limit in POL-007. The rental property mortgage RNT-4 has a maximum LTV of 75% (see PRD-006). The self-employed mortgage SEA-5 has a maximum LTV of 75% (see PRD-007). The home equity line of credit HEL-1 has a maximum combined LTV of 65% (see PRD-005). Combined LTV is the first mortgage balance plus the HEL-1 credit limit, divided by the property value. These product limits apply instead of the conventional limit. They are maximums even where mortgage default insurance would otherwise be available, so a borrower using one of these products may not exceed the product limit by insuring the loan. A file that exceeds a product limit needs an approved exception under POL-010.
