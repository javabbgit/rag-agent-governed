# Borrower FAQ: Property Tax and Home Insurance

Answers to common questions about property tax and home insurance on your Demo Mortgage Co. mortgage: the property tax account is optional, and proof of home insurance is required at closing and at each renewal.

## Do I have to open a property tax account?

No. A property tax account with Demo Mortgage Co. is optional. It is your choice whether to have one, and you can get your mortgage without one. If you do not have a property tax account, you remain responsible for paying the property tax on your home yourself. Property tax is counted in your housing costs when Demo Mortgage Co. tests your application against the ratio limits in POL-003, whether or not you have a property tax account, so choosing or not choosing an account does not change how your application is tested. If you would like to know whether a property tax account suits you, ask Customer Care. Staff will explain your choices but cannot tell you which one is better for you, because that depends on your own plans.

## Do I need home insurance on my property?

Yes. Demo Mortgage Co. requires proof of home insurance on your property for your mortgage. You must give proof of home insurance at closing, and again at each renewal. Proof of home insurance is one of the standard conditions that Underwriting attaches to an approval (see PRC-003). It is also on the document checklist for every type of file, whether you are buying a home, refinancing, or borrowing against a rental property. The proof is a document from your insurer that shows the property is covered. Your broker or branch can tell you exactly which document to send. Mortgage Operations will not release the funds for your mortgage until the proof is in your file and has been signed off.

## When do I have to give proof of home insurance for closing?

Proof of home insurance is a condition of your approval, and every condition must be met, with the supporting document in the loan system, at least 10 business days before your closing date (see PRC-003). Your approval states the exact due date for each condition. Send the proof to your broker or branch in good time, because a document that arrives after the due date may mean that your closing has to be moved. Staff cannot tell you that a late condition will not affect your closing. Before the funds are released, Mortgage Operations also checks that the proof of home insurance is in your file as part of its final conditions check (see PRC-006).

## Who checks my proof of home insurance?

Mortgage Operations checks and signs off the proof of home insurance. Each condition on an approval is signed off by one role, and the role for home insurance proof is Mortgage Operations (see PRC-003). The person who checks your proof cannot be the person who supplied it. Mortgage Operations records the date of the sign-off in the loan system. Underwriting signs off other conditions, such as the appraisal and your income documents, but not the proof of home insurance. If Mortgage Operations finds a problem with your proof, your broker or branch will be told what is missing so that you can send a new document. The sign-off is recorded in the loan system, and Mortgage Operations does not change a condition on its own.

## What happens if I do not give proof of home insurance?

If the proof of home insurance is not in your file by the due date, the condition is open. Your approval stays approved with conditions until the condition is met, the approval lapses or the closing date passes (see PRC-003). Underwriting cannot waive a condition. If the condition is still open at closing, Mortgage Operations cannot release the funds (see PRC-006). Your broker or branch will be told which condition is open and what will happen next. The way to avoid delay is to send the proof as soon as your insurance is in place. A rate hold does not protect you from delay, because a rate hold is not an approval and it ends on its own date (see RATE-003).

## Do I have to show proof of home insurance when I renew?

Yes. You must give current proof of home insurance at each renewal. This applies even to a straight renewal, which is a renewal with the same borrowers, for no more than the balance you owe and with no change to the property (see PRC-007). A straight renewal is not requalified, so Demo Mortgage Co. does not test your income and debts again, but it still needs the current proof of home insurance. Your renewal notice comes 120 days before your maturity date, which gives you time to arrange the proof. Your broker or branch can tell you which document to send. Staff cannot promise that a renewal will be approved, because a straight renewal is still a credit decision (see PRC-007).
