# Self-Employed Borrowers

POL-008 sets the rules for borrowers who work for themselves. They need at least 2 years in business, their income is the 2-year average of net income, and they must supply notices of assessment for both years. SEA-5 is the product option.

## Who counts as a self-employed borrower

A self-employed borrower is a borrower who owns the business that pays them, or who owns more than half of a corporation that pays them, or who is paid mainly by commission or contract with no regular employer. A borrower who owns less than half of a corporation and receives a regular salary and pay stub from it is treated as salaried and verified under POL-002. Underwriting must decide at application which type applies and must record the decision in the file. If the borrower has both salaried and self-employed income, Underwriting verifies the salaried income under POL-002 and the self-employed income under POL-008, and each is counted separately. A borrower who cannot show how the income is earned is not treated as self-employed and the income may not be counted.

## Minimum time in business

A self-employed borrower must have been in business for at least 2 years before the application. The 2 years are counted from the start of the business to the date of the application, and the Underwriter must confirm the start date from a business registration, an incorporation document or the earliest notice of assessment. A borrower who has changed from one business to another in the same field may count the time in the earlier business if the Underwriter can show that the work and the clients are the same. A borrower who has been self-employed for less than 2 years may not use self-employed income to qualify. If the borrower has a salaried income that can be verified under POL-002, the file may proceed on that income alone.

## Income: the 2-year average of net income

Underwriting must count self-employed income as the average net income of the last 2 years. Net income is the income shown on the notice of assessment after business expenses are deducted. Gross business revenue is never counted as income. The Underwriter adds the net income of each of the 2 years and divides by 2. If the net income of the most recent year is lower than the average, the Underwriter must use the lower figure. If the business has made a loss in either year, the Underwriter may not count self-employed income. Retained earnings of a corporation are not income unless the borrower has taken them as salary or dividends shown on the notice of assessment. The resulting figure is used in the debt service ratios in POL-003.

## Documents required for self-employed income

A self-employed borrower must supply notices of assessment for the last 2 years and the matching income tax returns. Each notice of assessment must show that the tax was assessed and that there is no unpaid balance, or must be supported by a statement showing that the balance is on a payment plan. The Underwriter must compare the net income on the returns with the net income on the notices and with the borrower's bank statements, and must resolve any difference before deciding. For a corporation, the borrower must also supply a document showing ownership and the corporation's financial statements for the same 2 years. A borrower who cannot supply notices of assessment for both years may not use self-employed income. A request to accept other documents is an exception request and must follow POL-010 as an income-document exception.

## The SEA-5 option for self-employed borrowers

Demo Mortgage Co. offers a product for self-employed borrowers called the Self-Employed Advantage Mortgage, SEA-5 (see PRD-007). SEA-5 is a 5-year fixed rate mortgage with a maximum LTV of 75%. A self-employed borrower may also apply for any other Demo Mortgage Co. product for which they qualify, and the income rules in POL-008 apply whichever product is chosen. The choice of SEA-5 does not change the income documents, the 2-year average or the minimum time in business. Underwriting qualifies every SEA-5 file at the qualifying rate in POL-004 and against the ratio limits in POL-003. The Underwriter records in the file which product the borrower chose and why SEA-5 or another product was selected.
